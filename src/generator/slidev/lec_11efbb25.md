<h1 style="color: #e2b96f;">1. The Cultural and Moral Significance of Speech</h1>

- Speech culture reflects inner world and moral character.
- Parents and educators share responsibility for early speech.
- Correct pronunciation and grammar build strong communication skills.
- Vocabulary richness and eloquence earn respect among peers.
- Play, stories, and nature themes expand children's imagination.
---

<h1 style="color: #e2b96f;">2. Core Objectives of Speech Readiness Activities</h1>

- Correct pronunciation of speech sounds.
- Expand, strengthen, and activate vocabulary.
- Ensure grammatically correct speech structure.
- Focus on speech fluency and smoothness.
- Prepare children for school education.
---

<h1 style="color: #e2b96f;">2.1. Foundational Skills: Correct Pronunciation, Vocabulary Expansion, and Grammar</h1>

- Teach correct pronunciation of speech sounds.
- Expand vocabulary through active reinforcement.
- Ensure grammatically correct speech structures.
- Focus on smoothness and logical sentence flow.
- Prepare children effectively for school learning.
---

<h1 style="color: #e2b96f;">2.2. Advanced Skills: Fluency, Logical Structure, and Independent Storytelling</h1>

<h1 style="color: #e2b96f;">3. The Shared Responsibility of Parents and Educators</h1>

- Parents and educators share the responsibility for speech.
- Caregivers must explain meanings during word learning games.
- Educators must model correct speech culture in every lesson.
- Shared efforts ensure children master vocabulary and grammar.
- Collaboration builds a rich, accurate, and fluent language.
